Summarize

Summarize

  • Although we store data about many observations…
  • …we often want to summarize across observations
    • This is like folding the tibble down to one row
  • We have seen functions that summarize vectors
    • length(), sum(), min(), max()
    • mean(), median(), sd(), var()
  • summarize() lets us use them on tibbles
    • It works very similarly to mutate()
    • It always creates a tibble as output

1. Preparing the Data

First, let’s create a small example dataset of sales to practice our summary functions.

library(tidyverse)

sales <- 
  tibble(
    customer = c(1, 2, 3, 1, 3),
    store = c("A", "A", "A", "B", "B"),
    items = c(25, 20, 16, 10, 5),
    spent = c(685, 590, 392, 185, 123)
  )

sales
# A tibble: 5 × 4
  customer store items spent
     <dbl> <chr> <dbl> <dbl>
1        1 A        25   685
2        2 A        20   590
3        3 A        16   392
4        1 B        10   185
5        3 B         5   123

2. Basic Summarize

We can use summarize() to calculate the average items and spent across all observations in the dataset.

sales |> 
  summarize(
    avg_items = mean(items),
    avg_spent = mean(spent)
  )
# A tibble: 1 × 2
  avg_items avg_spent
      <dbl>     <dbl>
1      15.2       395

3. The summary() Pitfall

Warning: Be careful not to accidentally use base R’s summary() function when you mean to use dplyr::summarize(). The former will not return a tidy tibble.

sales |> 
  summary(
    avg_items = mean(items),
    avg_spent = mean(spent)
  )
    customer    store               items          spent    
 Min.   :1   Length:5           Min.   : 5.0   Min.   :123  
 1st Qu.:1   Class :character   1st Qu.:10.0   1st Qu.:185  
 Median :2   Mode  :character   Median :16.0   Median :392  
 Mean   :2                      Mean   :15.2   Mean   :395  
 3rd Qu.:3                      3rd Qu.:20.0   3rd Qu.:590  
 Max.   :3                      Max.   :25.0   Max.   :685

4. Counting Functions

We can also use special functions like n() to count total rows, and n_distinct() to count unique values.

sales |> 
  summarize(
    n_sales = n(),
    n_customers = n_distinct(customer),
    n_stores = n_distinct(store)
  )
# A tibble: 1 × 3
  n_sales n_customers n_stores
    <int>       <int>    <int>
1       5           3        2

Grouped Summarize

Grouped Summarize

  • We can also summarize a tibble by group
    • This is like folding the tibble multiple times
    • Specifically, we fold down to one row per group
  • We just add the .by argument to summarize()
    • We can group by one or many variables
    • With many, we group by their combinations

1. Summarizing by One Group

Let’s see how the sales perform on a per-store basis by adding the .by argument.

sales |> 
  summarize(
    customers = n_distinct(customer),
    items_sold = sum(items),
    total_sales = sum(spent),
    avg_items = mean(items),
    avg_spent = mean(spent),
    .by = store
  )
# A tibble: 2 × 6
  store customers items_sold total_sales avg_items avg_spent
  <chr>     <int>      <dbl>       <dbl>     <dbl>     <dbl>
1 A             3         61        1667      20.3      556.
2 B             2         15         308       7.5      154

2. Summarizing by Multiple Groups

If we pass multiple variables to .by, it summarizes every unique combination of those groups.

sales |> 
  summarize(
    n_visits = n(),
    items_bought = sum(items),
    total_spent = sum(spent),
    .by = c(customer, store)
  )
# A tibble: 5 × 5
  customer store n_visits items_bought total_spent
     <dbl> <chr>    <int>        <dbl>       <dbl>
1        1 A            1           25         685
2        2 A            1           20         590
3        3 A            1           16         392
4        1 B            1           10         185
5        3 B            1            5         123

4. Flight Delays by Carrier

We can find the carrier with the lowest average departure delays by grouping and sorting. Note that we need na.rm = TRUE to ignore missing values.

flights |> 
  summarize(
    m_delay = mean(dep_delay, na.rm = TRUE),
    .by = carrier
  ) |> 
  arrange(m_delay) |> 
  head()
# A tibble: 6 × 2
  carrier m_delay
  <chr>     <dbl>
1 US         3.78
2 HA         4.90
3 AS         5.80
4 AA         8.59
5 DL         9.26
6 MQ        10.6

5. Busiest Days of the Year

By grouping by both month and day, we get 365 rows instead of just 31 (which would happen if we only grouped by day).

flights |> 
  summarize(
    n_flights = n(),
    .by = c(month, day)
  ) |> 
  arrange(desc(n_flights)) |> 
  head()
